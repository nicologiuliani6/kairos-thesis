\chapter*{Abstract}
Kairos is a \emph{reversible} and \emph{concurrent} programming language,
inspired by Janus~\cite{YokoyamaGluck2007} and extended to express concurrent
problems. Every Kairos program can be executed both forwards and backwards, and
its inverse is computed from the program text. The language offers explicit
parallelism, synchronous communication over typed channels and transactions
with compensation.

This thesis gives its structural operational semantics in small-step style,
defined as an extension of the semantics of Janus by Lami, Lanese and
Stefani~\cite{LamiLaneseStefani2024}, and the inversion operator that realises
backward execution. On channels we adopt a binary session discipline, checked
during execution, which allows delegation, that is, the passing of a channel
from one holder to another.
The language is implemented by an interpreter, with a debugger that runs the
program one step at a time, forwards and backwards. A case study, the
reversible Burrows--Wheeler transform with the blocks transformed in
parallel, measures its cost and the speedup obtained from concurrency.
